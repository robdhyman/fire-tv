# Benchmark: Netflix on connected TV, desktop and mobile

Resolves [#7](https://github.com/robdhyman/fire-tv/issues/7). Researched 2 Nov 2026 against Netflix's own sources (Tech Blog, Help Centre, about.netflix.com, Tudum) with press coverage only where Netflix has published nothing. Vocabulary follows [GLOSSARY.md](../../GLOSSARY.md); "billboard" appears only inside Netflix quotes. "P" marks a primary (Netflix-owned) source, "S" a secondary one. Claims we could not trace to a source are marked UNVERIFIED rather than guessed.

## Summary

Netflix's TV app is a single JavaScript UI on a custom renderer ("Gibbon") that treats focus as app state: one focus path, declarative `focused` props, nearest-neighbour spatial navigation, and a hard rule that focus must never be lost or doubled because "the user will think the app has frozen". Everything on the home screen is personalised and A/B tested: row choice and order, tile order, and the artwork itself. The May 2025 TV redesign moved navigation from a left sidebar to an always-visible top bar (Search, Shows, Movies, Games, My Netflix), made tiles expand in place with synopsis, runtime and callouts instead of updating a hero at the top, and made rows respond to browsing in real time. Playback behaviour is remarkably uniform across device classes: open controls, pick Audio & Subtitles, skip 10 s, Skip Intro when offered, autoplay the next episode with an "Are you still watching?" gate after three episodes. The device classes differ mainly in input (D-pad focus and voice on TV, hover cards and keyboard shortcuts on desktop, gestures, downloads, speed and clips on mobile), not in information architecture.

## Detailed findings

### Home and hero

- 2025 TV redesign (P, 7 May 2025): Search and My List shortcuts move "from the left-hand side to the top of the page"; title info gets callouts like "Emmy Award Winner" and "#1 in TV Shows"; recommendations respond "in the moment". [about.netflix.com](https://about.netflix.com/en/news/unveiling-our-innovative-new-tv-experience). Tudum (P) adds the always-visible shortcuts Search, Shows, Movies, Games, My Netflix; rollout from 19 May 2025 on "most TVs"; devices below the technical bar keep the left-menu layout. [Tudum](https://www.netflix.com/tudum/articles/netflix-new-tv-layout). The Help Centre confirms both layouts coexist and that Back from the homepage "returns you to the top menu". [N/321880164349028](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/321880164349028).
- Press briefing detail (S, Engadget 7 May 2025): tiles are "much larger"; the highlighted title's information no longer appears at the top of the screen but beside the tile; Back moves focus to the top bar; live events sit at the top of Home. [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/entertainment/streaming/netflix-overhauls-its-tv-app-with-a-fresh-ui-and-responsive-recommendations-121511958.html). Netflix's TV product lead on the old layout: members were "doing gymnastics with their eyes" scanning home. [Digital Trends, Jun 2024](https://digitaltrends.com/?p=3618937).
- Pre-2025 layout (P, Apr 2021): left-hand menu with the profile name at top and Play Something beneath it, also on the 10th row. [about.netflix.com](https://about.netflix.com/en/news/play-something-netflix-does-the-work-for-you).
- Home is built, not designed: rows are candidate groups filtered, ranked, selected for diversity, then "format[ted] ... to the appropriate size for the device". Members "are more likely to scan vertically than horizontally", so the upper left is the most-seen position. Placing the most tailored rows higher measurably improves retention. [Learning a Personalized Homepage, 2015](https://netflixtechblog.com/learning-a-personalized-homepage-aa8ec670359a); [Beyond the 5 Stars, 2012](https://netflixtechblog.com/netflix-recommendations-beyond-the-5-stars-part-1-55838468f429).
- Mobile (P): bottom bar Home, Clips, Search, My Netflix; top shortcuts to filter Shows/Movies/Games/Podcasts; New & Hot holds Coming Soon, Everyone's Watching, Top 10. [N/575087423404644](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/575087423404644). The 2023 iPhone refresh added an accelerometer parallax hero and art-derived gradients (S). [9to5mac](https://9to5mac.com/?p=858165).

### Browse rows, focus scaling and auto-preview

- Focus model (P, 2017): LRUD input; "Maintaining the correct focus for a single element is essential ... If focus gets in a bad state ... the user will think the app has frozen." Three systems: declarative focus (`focused` and `focusKey` props forming a path like `/app/menu/play`), spatial navigation (nearest neighbour in the pressed direction, containers can override), and focus requests (one focus path in app state). Pointer remotes need large targets; on return to LRUD, focus is restored to the last-focused or screen-default item. [Pass the Remote](https://netflixtechblog.com/pass-the-remote-user-input-on-tv-devices-923f6920c9a8).
- Performance (P, 2017): targets are key-input responsiveness, time to interactivity, FPS and memory on "sub-GHz single core CPUs"; techniques include pre-mounting screens, pooling list components and avoiding refs. [Crafting a High-Performance TV UI](https://netflixtechblog.com/crafting-a-high-performance-tv-user-interface-using-react-3350e5a6ad3b). Earlier: devices grouped into performance classes with animation and effects as knobs; a 1080p scene surface costs ~8 MB on devices with as little as 20 MB of render cache. [Building the New Netflix Experience for TV, 2013](https://netflixtechblog.com/building-the-new-netflix-experience-for-tv-920d71d875de).
- Auto-preview (P, Dec 2016): previews are "specially designed video synopses", not trailers, because Netflix has "less than 90 seconds to capture someone's attention"; testing showed members "browse less and watch more". [about.netflix.com](https://about.netflix.com/en/news/new-netflix-tv-experience-includes-video-previews-that-speed-your-next-selection). Browse-time video uses the same imperative player as playback, wrapped declaratively. [Integrating Imperative APIs](https://netflixtechblog.com/integrating-imperative-apis-into-a-react-application-1257e1b45ac6). Opt-out added Feb 2020 after public backlash (S, [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/02/06/netflixs-horrible-autoplay-previews-can-be-turned-off/)); setting is per profile; "iPads and some older TVs and TV streaming devices don't support preview autoplay". [N/2102](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/2102).
- 2025 tiles (S, Jun 2024 test coverage): tiles "extend as soon as your remote lands on them"; the preview plays inside the enlarged tile; genre, episodes, rating and duration sit beneath, synopsis one step lower; the added motion "may affect older TVs". [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/netflix-tv-app-redesign-3449668/).
- Desktop hover (S, Feb 2023): preview starts within ~2 s; the thumbs control fans out three options on hover and the author hit Dislike by accident. [Pratt IXD critique](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2023/02/design-critique-netflix).
- Artwork is personalised per member with contextual bandits, with exploration damped "such that artwork selections do not change too often" and no clickbait (rewarded on quality of engagement). Images with "expressive facial emotion that conveys the tone of the title do particularly well"; the brain processes an image in ~13 ms and members look at art before text. [Artwork Personalization, 2017](https://netflixtechblog.com/artwork-personalization-c589f074ad76); [Selecting the Best Artwork, 2016](https://netflixtechblog.com/selecting-the-best-artwork-for-videos-through-a-b-testing-f6155c4595f6). Artwork tests have yielded "20% to 30% more viewing for that title". [Experimentation Platform, 2016](https://netflixtechblog.com/its-all-a-bout-testing-the-netflix-experimentation-platform-4e1ca458c15). Double Thumbs Up sits beside thumbs up/down on every device class (P, Apr 2022). [about.netflix.com](https://about.netflix.com/en/news/two-thumbs-up-even-better-recommendations).

### Title page and episode model

- TV: "Add to My List" is reached by selecting a title and going down; AD and SDH badges on the details page gained text-to-speech in Oct 2022. [N/10523](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/10523); [N/119359](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/119359). Netflix's 2013 design premise: each title "has a tone and a narrative that should be conveyed by the UI ... we provide relevant evidence and cinematic art". [Tech Blog, 2013](https://netflixtechblog.com/building-the-new-netflix-experience-for-tv-920d71d875de).
- Mobile: Play and Download buttons enlarged (Jan 2022); season selector became a button (Nov 2021). [N/119359](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/119359).
- Desktop: a 2023 critique notes season 1 lists first when viewers often want the latest. [Pratt](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2023/02/design-critique-netflix). Exact TV episode-list layout (thumbnail, duration, progress bar) is not documented by Netflix: UNVERIFIED.

### Player chrome, 10 s skip, Skip Intro, post-play

- Opening controls: tap or click on phone, tablet, computer; "Press the up or down arrow on the remote" on TV; then "Audio & Subtitles at the top or bottom of the screen". [N/372](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/372).
- Keyboard (web): Enter/Space play-pause; F full screen; Esc exit; Left/Right rewind/fast-forward 10 s; Up/Down volume; M mute; S skip intro. [N/24855](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/24855). Playback speed is web and mobile only, 0.5x to 1.5x, re-chosen per title, never shipped to TV, not available on the ad plan in a browser. [N/116584](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/116584); [Player Control Tests, 2019](https://about.netflix.com/en/news/player-control-tests).
- Skip Intro (P, Mar 2022): members manually jumped ahead in the first five minutes "about 15% of the time"; shipped to web (2017), TV Aug 2017, mobile May 2018; design rule: "appear on screen only when needed and ... work with a single click"; pressed ~136 million times a day. [about.netflix.com](https://about.netflix.com/en/news/looking-back-on-the-origin-of-skip-intro-five-years-later). Skip Recap has no Netflix documentation: UNVERIFIED.
- Post-play: for a series, "Watch Credits" is offered at the end of an episode; for a film or final episode "credits shrink into a smaller window, and Netflix suggests what to watch next"; picking anything else removes the Watch Credits option. [N/114058](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/114058). Autoplay Next Episode is a per-profile toggle. [N/121518](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/121518). Countdown length is not stated anywhere by Netflix (press cites 10 or 15 s): UNVERIFIED.
- "Are you still watching?": on TVs "after three episodes or 90 minutes of continuous viewing"; elsewhere after three episodes with no control used. [N/114059](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/114059). Screensaver after 5 idle minutes, rotating titles every 15 s. [N/116953](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/116953). No autoplay on mobile networks. [N/100365](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/100365).
- Mobile extras: screen lock padlock (Android/iOS), PiP, Clip button (Moments, up to 2 minutes, saved to My Netflix). [N/115018](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/115018); [N/210664027435620](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/210664027435620).

### My List, Remind Me and Continue Watching

- Add: TV select title then "Add to My List"; mobile My List icon; web hover icon. Remove on TV: Back opens the menu, My Netflix, My List row, "Remove from My List". Cap 2,000 titles; default sort is relevance ("recently added, a new season available ... or a title leaving Netflix soon"); mobile adds sort (Date Added, A–Z, Release Date) and filters (Haven't Started, Started). [N/10523](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/10523).
- Remind Me on future-dated titles; set reminders appear in a "Reminders You've Set" row in My Netflix; on release the title is added to My List. [N/101522](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/101522).
- Continue Watching removal (Feb 2022): TV via the title's page menu, "Remove from Continue Watching"; web and mobile "Remove From Row". [N/115312](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/115312). Hidden titles take up to 24 h to leave all devices. [N/22205](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/22205).
- My Netflix (P, Jul 2023, mobile): "a one-stop shop" for downloads, thumbs-ups, saves, trailers watched, reminders and in-progress titles. [about.netflix.com](https://about.netflix.com/en/news/introducing-my-netflix-a-one-stop-shop-for-series-and-movies-you-want-to). On TV (2025) it holds Continue Watching, My List and Remind Me. [Tudum](https://www.netflix.com/tudum/articles/netflix-new-tv-layout).

### Search and on-screen keyboard

- Search icon on every device; searchable by title, people, genre, language, AD language and quality ("Atmos, UHD"). [N/47765](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/47765). On the 2025 TV layout the search screen offers "a list on the left" of categories plus typed search; the Categories shortcut was removed. [N/321880164349028](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/321880164349028). The A–Z grid keyboard layout is not documented by Netflix: UNVERIFIED.
- Voice: Alexa, Siri, Google Assistant, Bixby; "Find Bird Box on Netflix", "Play The Irishman on Netflix", pause/play/fast forward/rewind. [N/111997](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/111997). On Fire TV, Home and the microphone are system keys; the Netflix remote button cannot be intercepted; apps must handle D-pad because media keys may be absent. [Amazon: Remote Control Input](https://developer.amazon.com/docs/fire-tv/remote-input.html).
- Conversational search is an opt-in iOS beta. [N/349963919363172](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/349963919363172).

### Captions and audio description

- Four steps on every device: play, open controls, Audio & Subtitles, pick a track. AD is an audio track with "Audio Description" in its name; AD badge on the details page; `netflix.com/browse/audio-description`. [N/25079](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/25079). AD launched Apr 2015 with Daredevil. [about.netflix.com](https://about.netflix.com/en/news/netflix-begins-audio-description-for-visually-impaired).
- Appearance: font, size, shadow, background colour on web; TV via pause, Settings, "Size and Style"; "Connected TV devices don't offer all the subtitle appearance options available on the web"; if Settings is missing the device cannot change appearance. [N/100267](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/100267). Downloads carry only the two most relevant languages. [N/372](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/372).

### Motion and transition idiom

- 2013: transitions animate whole scenes on GPU surfaces; a pluggable effect pipeline (blur, desaturation, tint); animation gated by device performance class. [Tech Blog 2013](https://netflixtechblog.com/pioneering-application-design-on-tvs-tv-connected-devices-e361dbe02f66). 2017: FPS is a first-class metric; screens are pre-mounted "to improve perceived transition time". [Tech Blog 2017](https://netflixtechblog.com/crafting-a-high-performance-tv-user-interface-using-react-3350e5a6ad3b).
- 2023 TV motion refresh (S): profile pick zooms into the avatar, UI zooms in and out where it previously faded; a per-profile "Reduce animation effects when navigating on TV" setting reduces but does not remove motion. [What's on Netflix](https://www.whats-on-netflix.com/news/netflix-introduces-new-animations-in-tv-app-how-to-turn-them-off/); help node 130349 could not be rendered: UNVERIFIED wording.
- 2025: "animations between tiles are snappier and more fluid" (Netflix-attributed, S). [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/entertainment/streaming/netflix-overhauls-its-tv-app-with-a-fresh-ui-and-responsive-recommendations-121511958.html). Specific durations (focus scale ms, preview delay on TV) are not published: UNVERIFIED.

### Accessibility

- Netflix's list: screen readers, AD, subtitle styling, voice commands, assistive listening, mobile brightness/font size/speed, web keyboard shortcuts. On TV it names no reader, only "built-in screen readers". [N/116022](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/116022); [N/116023](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/116023). Fire TV VoiceView is never mentioned by Netflix: UNVERIFIED.
- Release notes show the TV accessibility backlog: missing speech on buttons and title details (Apr 2021), Kids browsing (Mar 2021), Continue Watching row (Jan 2022), speech stopping when backgrounded, AD/SDH badges spoken (Oct 2022). Mobile: Dynamic Type across Home and title pages; trailer autoplay disabled when VoiceOver runs; rows exposed as headings on Android. [N/119359](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/119359). Play Something supports TTS. [about.netflix.com](https://about.netflix.com/en/news/play-something-netflix-does-the-work-for-you).
- Netflix does not document Reduce Motion, high contrast, or TV text-size support beyond the 2023 animation toggle.

### What differs per device class

- TV only: D-pad focus with in-place tile expansion and in-tile preview; always-visible top bar with Games; Back opens the menu; Play Something under the profile name; voice remotes; 90-minute rule for "Are you still watching?"; no playback speed; Up/Down reveals controls; 5-minute screensaver.
- Desktop only: hover card with preview; keyboard shortcuts including S; "Browse by Languages"; account-level settings pages (autoplay, subtitle appearance, viewing activity).
- Mobile only: downloads and Smart Downloads inside My Netflix; Clips feed; Moments clipping; mobile games; speed, brightness, screen lock, PiP, double-tap skip; parallax hero; Dynamic Type; conversational search beta.

## Worth copying for the FireTV app

1. Focus as state: one focus path, declarative `focused` props, nearest-neighbour spatial navigation, and restore-last-focus on return. Never let focus vanish or double; that reads as a frozen app.
2. Expand the tile in place (2025 idiom) rather than repainting the hero on every focus move; show logline, runtime and a badge beside the tile so the eye stays put.
3. Back from Home moves focus to the top bar; Up/Down reveals player controls; Left/Right skips 10 s. Keep the whole app operable from the D-pad plus Back, since Fire TV media keys may be absent.
4. Skip Intro rule: show only when there is something to skip, one press, then disappear. Apply the same rule to the Up Next countdown and offer "Watch Credits".
5. A gate against runaway autoplay: pause Up Next after three episodes or 90 minutes and ask.
6. Four-step captions and AD path from the player (controls, Audio & Subtitles, track), AD as a named audio track, and AD/SDH badges on the show page with spoken labels.
7. Pre-mount the next screen and treat frames per second and key-to-render latency as metrics; gate heavier motion behind a device class and a "reduce animation" setting.
8. Put the most useful rows top-left: Up Next first, then the row most tailored to the viewer; label rows with their evidence ("because you watched").
9. Art that conveys tone with expressive faces; keep artwork stable across sessions so a show stays recognisable.

## Worth avoiding

1. Auto-preview on focus by default. Netflix had to add an opt-out after backlash, disables it under VoiceOver, and the heavier 2025 motion is reported to tax older hardware. Default off on Fire TV sticks, or make it dwell-triggered and silent.
2. Hiding "+ Add to My List" below the fold of a tile or show page; 2025 users complain it now needs a scroll. Keep `+` on the hero and show page as the glossary specifies.
3. Fan-out controls under a single focus target (desktop thumbs menu) that cause mis-presses; on a D-pad every control is one focusable item.
4. Netflix-style silent countdown with no published duration; our Up Next card states 15 s and shows it.
5. Playback speed and brightness on TV: Netflix deliberately never shipped them to the 10-foot UI.
6. Relying on built-in screen readers without naming them: Netflix's TV accessibility notes are a trail of missing speech. Test VoiceView on Fire TV from the first build.
7. Season 1 first on a show page for a viewer mid-way through a later season; open on the season of the Up Next episode.

## Open questions

- Netflix never publishes the post-play countdown length, the TV preview dwell delay or focus-scale timing; our 15 s Up Next and 200 ms focus scale are our own choices and should be checked in a user session.
- Which Fire TV generations count as the "device class" that keeps heavier motion (Netflix keeps the old layout on devices below an unpublished bar)?
- Does VoiceView on Fire TV announce our tile labels and badges, given Netflix's own backlog of unspoken controls?
- Does Up Next belong at the top of Home for every viewer, or only once they have a started episode (Netflix's Jun 2024 test put Continue Watching third, after a preview row and Top Picks)?
- Is a Play Something-style "pick for me" button worth a slot under the profile name for a two-brand catalogue of this size?
